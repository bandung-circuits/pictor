# tricon-infographic

Clean, authoritative research publication style with bold red accents on white — inspired by the visual identity of progressive Global South research institutes and political publications.

## Color Palette

- Primary: Tricontinental Red (#C41E3A) for headings, data labels, accents, and emphasis
- Background: Clean white (#FFFFFF), generous negative space
- Text: Black (#1A1A1A) for body content, dark gray (#4A4A4A) for secondary text
- Accents: Deep crimson (#8B1A2B) for depth, warm gold (#D4A843) for occasional secondary highlights
- Data colors: When multiple series are needed, use red as primary, then muted teal (#2A7B88), dark amber (#B8860B), slate blue (#4A6FA5), and olive (#6B8E23) — always with red dominant

## Visual Elements

- Clean horizontal rules as section dividers (thin red lines)
- Full-bleed red panels for emphasis sections (white text on solid red background)
- Generous white space — content breathes, nothing feels crowded
- Minimal ornamentation — let the data and text speak with authority

## Typography

- Bold condensed sans-serif for headlines and section titles (geometric, often uppercase or title case)
- Clean serif for body text (justified alignment, comfortable reading width)
- Red italic serif for captions and labels
- Small red sans-serif for metadata (dates, series labels, institutional attribution)

## Style Rules

**Do:**
- Use red as the single dominant accent color — it carries both urgency and authority
- Maintain generous margins and white space — this style signals "serious research," not "busy infographic"
- Let photographs and artwork take full-bleed space when used

**Don't:**
- Use decorative illustrations, icons, or clip-art — this is a publication, not a poster
- Use gradients, drop shadows, or glossy effects — keep surfaces flat and clean
- Crowd the canvas — every element needs breathing room
- Use more than 2-3 colors in any single composition — red + black + white is the core palette
- Add rounded corners, cartoon elements, or playful decorations — maintain editorial gravity
- Add section numbers (01, 02, etc.) — infographics are not magazine layouts

## Best For

Research findings, policy analysis, data-driven arguments, political economy, Global South perspectives, institutional reports, statistical comparisons, social movement documentation

## Register

- formal-casual: formal
- abstract-representational: slightly abstract
- bright-dark: bright
- digital-handcrafted: digital
- cultural-anchor: progressive research institute publication / political editorial design (Tricontinental, Verso, NACLA tradition)
- best-for: research findings, data-driven analysis, institutional reports, political economy content
- avoid-for: children's content, entertainment, product marketing, casual social media
